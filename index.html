        How the score works

        Quick check runs in your browser and nothing leaves the page. It measures how much your sentence lengths vary, how often you use phrases common in AI text ("delve", "it is important to note"), and how personal the voice is (contractions, "I").

        Analyze with AI sends the text to Claude (Anthropic's AI model), which reads each sentence and explains what feels generated. The final score blends both, 70% AI and 30% statistics. Your text is used only to produce the result and is not stored by this site.

        If a sentence is flagged and you wrote it, that is common. Adding your own examples, opinions, and specific sources usually makes writing read as more human, and better.

        A free AI detector and plagiarism checker for students

        Second Read helps university and college students check an assignment before they submit it. Paste your essay or upload a Word (.docx) or PDF file, and Second Read highlights the sentences that read as AI-written, explains why, and suggests a more natural rewrite. It can also check your text for plagiarism by comparing your most distinctive sentences with published books and websites. It is free: you just sign in with Google, and your text is not stored.

        1. Check for AI writing

        Each sentence gets a likelihood score. Flagged lines are highlighted so you can see exactly what sounds machine-written, such as stock phrases, flat rhythm, or vague claims.

        2. Fix grammar & cite sources

        The writing assistant catches spelling, grammar and punctuation mistakes, unclear or wordy sentences and tone, rewrites in styles like formal or academic, and builds APA, MLA and Chicago citations.

        3. Check for plagiarism

        Second Read searches the web and book sources like Google Books, Project Gutenberg and the Internet Archive for sentences that match yours, and links to each source it finds.

        4. Revise and download

        Humanize just the flagged lines, or rewrite your full text into a second box you can copy or download as Word. You can also download a report with your scores, flagged sentences and sources to keep for your own records.

        Frequently asked questions

        Is Second Read free?

        Yes. Second Read is completely free for everyone. You sign in with your Google account, but there is no payment, no subscription and no word-count paywall.

        How accurate is the AI detector?

        No AI detector is perfect. Scores are a signal, not proof. Formal academic writing and writing by non-native English speakers are flagged more often, so use the results to revise your work, never as evidence against someone.

        Can it check a Word document or PDF?

        Yes. Click upload and choose a .docx, .pdf or .txt file. The text is read in your browser and placed in the box for checking.

        Does the plagiarism checker look at books?

        Yes. Along with websites, it searches book sources such as Google Books, Project Gutenberg and the Internet Archive, and marks each match as a book or a website.

        Does it check grammar like Grammarly?

        Yes. The writing assistant finds spelling, grammar and punctuation mistakes, flags wordy or unclear sentences, shows your tone, rewrites text in different styles (formal, academic, simpler, shorter and more), and makes APA, MLA and Chicago citations. It's free.

        Why do I need to sign in?

        Signing in with Google keeps the checker fair and free by limiting how many checks one person can run per minute. We only see your name and email address.

        Is my essay saved or shared?

        No. Your text is only used to produce your result and is not stored by this site.
