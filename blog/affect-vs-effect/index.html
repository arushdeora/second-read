Affect vs Effect: The Easy Way to Remember (With Examples)

"Affect" and "effect" sound almost the same, and they're about the same idea, so even good writers mix them up. Here's the rule, a memory trick that sticks, and the two exceptions you'll actually meet.

The short answer

Affect is usually a verb: to influence something.
Lack of sleep affects your memory.

Effect is usually a noun: the result.
Lack of sleep has an effect on your memory.

Why everyone mixes them up

There are three reasons. First, in normal speech the two words sound almost the same, so your ear can't help you. Second, they come in pairs in the same sentence: something affects you, and that has an effect. Third, "effect" can sometimes be a verb and "affect" can sometimes be a noun (more on that below), which makes people unsure even when they know the basic rule.

The mistake matters more than it looks. In an essay, report or exam answer it's one of the errors markers notice straight away. In a science or social science essay you may write about "the effects of X on Y" a dozen times, so getting it wrong once usually means getting it wrong everywhere.

The memory trick: A for Action, E for End result

  • Affect = Action (something you do: a verb)
  • Effect = End result (a thing: a noun)

Another popular trick is RAVEN: Remember, Affect is a Verb, Effect is a Noun.

A quick test you can use in any sentence

Look at the word just before it:

  • If there's a, an, the, any, no or an adjective (big, side, positive) before it, you need the noun: effect. "The side effects", "a big effect", "no effect."
  • If you could swap in "influence(s)" or "change(s)" as a verb, you need affect. "Stress affects (influences) sleep."

The two exceptions

1. "Effect" as a verb = to bring about

"Effect" can be a verb meaning to cause or make happen. It's mostly used in the phrase "effect change".

  • The student council wants to effect change in the canteen. (= make change happen)
  • The student council wants to affect change in the canteen. (= influence a change that's already happening)

In everyday essays, "effect change" is the one to know. Otherwise, stick with the main rule.

2. "Affect" as a noun = visible emotion

In psychology, "affect" (stress on the first syllable: AF-fect) is a noun for how someone's emotions look from outside: "The patient had a flat affect." Unless you're writing a psychology paper, you probably won't need it.

Other forms

  • Affected (verb, past): The flood affected thousands of homes.
  • Effective (adjective): An effective study plan. Note: "effective" comes from effect, the result.
  • Effectively (adverb): She managed her time effectively.
  • In effect (phrase): The rule is in effect from Monday. This is always "effect."

Fixing it in your own essay

Use your editor's search (Ctrl+F, or Cmd+F on a Mac) to find every "affect" and "effect" in your essay. For each one, ask: is this an action or a thing? Try putting "the" in front of it. If "the ___" makes sense, it's a noun, so effect. If "it will ___" makes sense, it's a verb, so affect. It takes two minutes and removes the mistake from the whole essay.

Exam tip: in board exams and university papers, phrases like "causes and effects", "effects of pollution" and "how it affects the economy" come up all the time. Learn those three as set phrases and you're covered in most answers.

Similar pairs that trip students up

If affect/effect gets you, these might too: than / then (comparison vs time), its / it's (possession vs "it is"), their / there / they're, principle / principal, and advice / advise (noun vs verb, the same pattern as effect/affect).

FAQ

Is it affect or effect? Affect is usually a verb meaning to influence (stress affects sleep). Effect is usually a noun meaning the result (stress has an effect on sleep).

What is an easy way to remember affect vs effect? A for Action, E for End result: affect is the action (verb), effect is the end result (noun). The RAVEN trick also works: Remember, Affect is a Verb, Effect is a Noun.

Is it 'affect change' or 'effect change'? Usually 'effect change', which means to bring change about. 'Affect change' means to influence a change that is already happening, and is much less common.

Can effect be a verb? Yes, but rarely. As a verb, effect means to cause or bring about, mainly in the phrase 'effect change'.
